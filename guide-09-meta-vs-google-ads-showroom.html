Meta Ads vs Google Ads for a Showroom: Which Drives More Walk-ins?

For a tile, stone, or furniture showroom, Meta and Google Ads aren't interchangeable line items in a budget. They catch a buyer at completely different points in their decision, and treating them the same wastes spend on both.

The same logic holds for real estate offices, clinics, auto and equipment dealers, and most B2B businesses where the sale closes after a walk-in or a serious conversation, not a click. Swap "showroom" for your business below and the reasoning still applies.

What each platform is actually good at

Google Ads catches people already searching — someone typing "granite showroom near me" has already decided they want granite, today. That's high intent, and it's why search ads usually convert faster for a showroom.

Meta Ads catches people who weren't searching for anything. It works on visual appeal and interruption — a beautiful finish, a before-and-after, a founder explaining a material choice. Nobody wakes up wanting to see your ad, so it has to earn attention on its own.

Why Google usually wins for showrooms with high purchase intent

A showroom purchase is considered and often expensive. By the time someone searches your category, they've usually done homework already. Google Ads meets that decision-stage buyer at the exact moment they're ready to act, which is why it tends to produce the most walk-ins per rupee for showroom categories.

Where Meta still earns its budget

  • Building the awareness and trust that gets someone searching for you by name later, rather than a generic category term
  • Reaching architects, designers, brokers, referral partners, and repeat buyers who aren't actively shopping right now but will be soon
  • Retargeting people who've already visited your website or profile, where a search-intent signal isn't needed because the interest already exists

Google Ads sells to people who already decided. Meta Ads decides people. A showroom usually needs both, in that order, not one instead of the other.

How to decide with your own numbers, not a generic rule

  1. Track walk-ins by source, not just clicks or leads, before deciding either platform "isn't working."
  2. Give Google Ads the search-intent budget first — it's usually the faster path to a confirmed sale for a showroom.
  3. Layer Meta in for awareness and retargeting once tracking can actually attribute a walk-in back to it, not before.
